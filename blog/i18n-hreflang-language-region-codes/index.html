Hreflang codes must name the live language URLs

Definition

A language code such as en or zh names a language. A region segment is optional and must match a page you really publish for that region. This site uses zh-Hans for the Chinese pages under /zh/ and does not invent an /en/ prefix. A code that points at a redirect, a 404, or a thin mirror relates a URL that is not the document. Self-references belong in the set so each page names itself as well as its alternate.

Short answer

Hreflang language and region codes tell a crawler which canonical URL is the alternate for a language or region. They work only when each listed URL returns that version, the annotations are reciprocal, and x-default points at the English URL. They do not assign rankings.

Fields that must say the same thing

Check the live canonical, not a staging host. The English canonical and the Chinese canonical should list each other. x-default should list the English URL. Codes and page language ought to agree: a zh-Hans annotation on an English-only body is a mismatch, not a market strategy.

The issue on the page

Hreflang does not translate the specification. If the codes are perfect and the Chinese page drops a unit, GEO Health still has an entity problem. SEO Growth may still have an uncovered question in that language. Fix the facts and the codes as separate findings.

Signals that travel with the page

The sitemap can repeat the alternates after the URLs exist. A sitemap entry is not a substitute for the tags on the page, and the tags are not a substitute for internal links. Orphan alternates remain hard to discover.

Hreflang is a language relation between documents, not a ranking control. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Confirm each hreflang URL returns 200 and the intended language.
  • Require a reciprocal annotation and a self-reference.
  • Point x-default at the English canonical.
  • Do not use hreflang to paper over a missing specification.

What this does not claim

Correct hreflang codes do not guarantee search rankings or AI citations. Mika does not claim that a region code will win a market.

A practical example

Teaching example, not a traffic report. A product URL in English annotates zh-Hans toward a URL that redirects to the homepage. The return tag does not exist. The repair points both annotations at the real canonicals, adds the self-reference and x-default, and re-audits the fetch. No position is promised.
