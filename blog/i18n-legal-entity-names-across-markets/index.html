Language versions should not invent a second company

Definition

Company information should say who you are, what you sell, and where you operate, without hype. If a language version translates the legal name into a different proper noun, summaries will treat the site as two organizations. Preferred and alternative labels can both appear when you say which one is the legal name. Do not borrow another firm’s name, logo, or award to fill the space.

Short answer

Across markets, the legal entity on the page should stay one named company. A trade name used in a market can be an alias, stated as an alias, not a second company with a second set of facts.

Fields that must say the same thing

Check company name, product name, specification, application, and claims in each language. A market page may add a real operating note, such as which entity contracts in that language, when that note is true. It may not delete the product limit to make the story shorter.

The issue on the page

Put the legal name in HTML on the about page and on the product page that needs it. A logo image without the name in text is a weak identifier. Link the two pages with the name as anchor text, inside the language the reader is using.

Signals that travel with the page

Hreflang relates the language versions of the same entity. It is the wrong tool for sending readers to an unrelated local distributor you have not identified in the sentence. x-default remains the English URL.

One company should survive translation as one entity with stable names. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Read the legal name on the English and Chinese about pages.
  • Label trade names as aliases.
  • Keep the product facts attached to that same entity.
  • Do not add customer or award logos you cannot support.

What this does not claim

Stable legal names do not guarantee search rankings, knowledge-panel placement, or AI citations. Mika does not invent corporate affiliations.

A practical example

Teaching example, not a filing. English says the sensors are sold by North Line Instruments. The other language invents a translated house name and drops “sold by.” The repair restores the legal name, and if a local trade name exists it adds “also styled as” plus that alias. No registry ranking is claimed.
