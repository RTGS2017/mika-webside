Noindex is an instruction about that URL, not a hint

Noindex tells a cooperating crawler not to keep this URL in its index. The URL can still be fetched. Do not also block that fetch in robots.txt if you need the directive to be seen. Do not noindex a URL you still want found.

Short answer

Noindex tells a cooperating crawler not to keep this URL in its index. The URL can still be fetched. Do not also block that fetch in robots.txt if you need the directive to be seen. Do not noindex a URL you still want found.

Definition

Noindex can live in a meta robots tag or in an HTTP header. It applies to the URL that returned it. A template that accidentally noindexes every product page is a template defect, not a copy edit. Canonical and noindex together need a conscious choice: a duplicate you do not want indexed can be noindexed, or it can canonicalize to the owner. Doing both at random leaves an unclear document.

What belongs on the page

Fetch the URL and read the robots directive in HTML and in headers. Confirm robots.txt does not disallow the URL you need fetched. Check a sample of the template, not one hero. Faceted or internal search URLs are common places for a deliberate noindex. Money pages are not.

What to check

  • Read noindex from the live response, not from a design file.
  • Do not disallow the same URL in robots.txt if noindex must be seen.
  • Fix the template when the directive is widespread.
  • Do not noindex the URL that owns the specification.

A practical example

A staging flag left noindex on the live valve family template. Every family URL asks not to be indexed. The repair removes the directive from the template, confirms the canonicals, and re-audits a sample of URLs. No recovery ranking is promised.

What this does not claim

Removing a mistaken noindex does not guarantee rankings or AI citations. Mika reports the directive. It does not promise reinstatement.
