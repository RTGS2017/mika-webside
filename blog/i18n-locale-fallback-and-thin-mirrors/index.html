Locale fallback must not publish a thin mirror

Definition

A thin mirror repeats a page without the market’s questions or without the full facts. That is a duplicate-content problem, not SEO growth. If you do not yet have a real Chinese page, do not publish an empty /zh/ shell and point hreflang at it. Keep the English document as the one canonical until the alternate has the same facts and real depth. x-default stays on English.

Short answer

Locale fallback can show a source-language string when a translation is missing inside an interface. It should not become a public URL that copies the other language, swaps a label, and pretends the market is covered.

Fields that must say the same thing

Decide per URL. Either the language version answers the buyer with the same company, product, specification, and application facts, or it is not ready to be an alternate. Fallback text inside a widget can remain unindexed. The indexable page is the document.

The issue on the page

Near-duplicates that only insert a city or language name into one paragraph are copies. The health response is to keep the real page and stop the copies. Growth, if you choose it, means writing the uncovered question, not minting URLs.

Signals that travel with the page

Internal links should not send a Chinese reader only to English for the specification and also advertise a Chinese canonical that has no specification. Pick one honest path, then re-audit.

Fallback is an interface behavior. A market page is a document with facts. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Do not hreflang a shell that only falls back to English text.
  • Keep one canonical until the alternate has the same facts.
  • Treat city-and-language copies as duplicates.
  • Re-audit the live URLs after the shells are removed.

What this does not claim

Refusing thin mirrors does not guarantee rankings or AI citations. Mika does not count unpublished locales as coverage.

A practical example

Teaching example, not a localization score. A menu offers Chinese, but every “Chinese” URL is the English paragraph plus a translated title, and hreflang treats it as zh-Hans. The repair removes those shells from the index and from hreflang until the pages carry the same limits and a real answer. No coverage percentage is claimed.
