Automotive localization keeps one part number in every language

Definition

A supplier page is useful when a buyer can match a drawing to a sentence. The sentence names the part number, the condition, and the limit. Translation may change word order. It may not invent a second limit, drop the unit, or replace the part number with a marketing name. English stays at the site root and Chinese stays under /zh/, with reciprocal hreflang and x-default on the English URL. Those signals point at the pages. They do not forgive a mismatched specification.

Short answer

Automotive localization means the same part number, the same operating range, and the same safety limit appear in every language you actually publish. A fluent page that changes a torque value is a different part, not a market version.

Fields that must say the same thing

Before you compare traffic, compare six fields: company name, part number, specification including the unit, application, the claim with its condition, and whether the buyer question is answered, partial, or uncovered in that language. A Chinese page that shortens the operating range is partial even when the template looks finished.

The issue on the page

In-language links should stay inside the language the reader is using. A Chinese paragraph that only points at the English drawing makes the Chinese URL depend on another language for the actual limit. That is a discovery problem and an answerability problem together. An orphan translation listed only in a sitemap is still hard to use.

Signals that travel with the page

Evidence for the limit is the drawing, the datasheet, or the standard named beside the sentence. Each language needs a working link to that evidence. Citation readiness here means a reader can tell which part the sentence describes. It is not a promise that any answer product will quote the page.

On an automotive part page, the entity is the part number, not the campaign line. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Read the part number and the unit aloud on the English and Chinese URLs.
  • Confirm the safety limit survived translation, including the condition.
  • Check reciprocal hreflang among the live canonicals, with x-default on English.
  • Follow one in-language link from the family page to the part page.

What this does not claim

A practical example

Teaching example, not a customer story. A brake-hose page in English states a pressure limit and a temperature range next to part number BH-240. The new language version keeps the headline and drops the temperature range. A buyer asking which hose matches a stated under-hood temperature gets a fluent page and the wrong constraint. The repair restores the range on that URL, links the drawing in the same language path, and re-audits. Nothing in that loop is a ranking forecast.
