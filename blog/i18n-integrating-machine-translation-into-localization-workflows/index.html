The issue on the page

Machine translation still needs a fact check

Machine translation can draft a language version. It cannot decide which facts are allowed to change. Numbers, model codes, ratio and torque rating, and the application name are the first places a draft goes wrong, because they look optional to a sentence-level system and they are not optional to a buyer. A human review of those fields is the publication gate. A fluent paragraph with a shifted rating is a worse page than a shorter accurate one.

Answer systems are sensitive to the disagreement, not to how the disagreement was produced. If the English page says rated for continuous duty inside the published torque band and the generated Chinese page drops the condition, both pages are now sources. A later summary may blend them into a claim the manufacturer never made. Citation readiness falls because a careful reader cannot tell which sentence is safe to quote.

Keep machine-translated drafts out of the index until the six fields match: company name, product name, specifications, applications, claims, and question coverage. Publishing the draft to “see what happens” adds a URL. It does not add SEO Growth. Growth is unoccupied intent that the site can honestly support, not an unreviewed mirror.

Signals that travel with the page

A subdomain splits the host. Links, cookies, and crawl paths no longer match the root by default. If you use one, the product facts still have to match the root language, and the language switcher has to land on the equivalent product URL rather than the subdomain homepage.

An XML sitemap can list each language URL and its alternates. It is a coverage hint. It does not force indexing, and it does not repair a page that disagrees with its alternate. Read the sitemap for mismatches: URLs in the file that canonicals point away from, and alternates that return an error.

What to check

  • Open the English and Chinese URLs for helical gear reducer side by side and read ratio and torque rating aloud. If one side is shorter, the fields do not match.
  • Confirm reciprocal hreflang among the live canonicals, including a self-reference, with x-default on the English URL.
  • Follow the in-language internal links from the hub to the product page so the alternate is not an orphan.
  • Read the direct answer to “Which reducer matches this conveyor torque?” and check that the condition in “rated for continuous duty inside the published torque band” survived translation.
