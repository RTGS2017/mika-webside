Quotes should name the currency, not only a symbol

Currency localization on a B2B quote means both languages name one currency for one amount. A symbol that two readers assign to two currencies is not yet a price fact.

Definition

Prices on product and quote pages are claims with a unit. The unit is the currency. Spell a code or a name that matches across languages, and say whether tax or shipping sits inside the figure. Translation must not convert the number unless you also show the original currency and the basis for the conversion. Inventing a local price the commercial page cannot support is a new claim.

Short answer

Currency localization on a B2B quote means both languages name one currency for one amount. A symbol that two readers assign to two currencies is not yet a price fact.

Fields that must say the same thing

Align company name, product name, the amount, the currency, and what the amount includes. Question coverage for “what does this quote include?” is answered only when those words are on the URL. A contact form is not a currency.

The issue on the page

Keep the sentence in HTML. An amount that exists only in a picture of a spreadsheet is easy to miss and easy to contradict. Link the quote or the price note with anchor text that includes the currency name.

Signals that travel with the page

Hreflang does not convert money. It relates the language URLs. If only one language has a price, do not pretend the other language answered the commercial question. Mark that question partial or uncovered.

A price without a currency is an unfinished specification. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Name the currency in words or a code on both language URLs.
  • Say what the amount excludes.
  • Do not convert a number without stating the basis.
  • Keep the amount in HTML text.

What this does not claim

Clear currency labels do not guarantee search rankings, conversions, or AI citations. Mika does not publish exchange-rate advice or placement promises.

A practical example

Teaching example, not a price index. An English spare-part row says 480 USD, excluding freight. The other language keeps 480 and a symbol the reader may assign to another currency, and it drops the freight exclusion. The repair writes the currency name and the exclusion in both bodies. No exchange rate is invented as a result.
