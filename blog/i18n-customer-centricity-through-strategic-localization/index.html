Customer-centric localization answers the buyer in that language

Definition

Buyers do not experience your glossary. They experience whether the page names the product, the application, and the constraint. Strategic here means you choose which questions are in scope for that market and you answer those questions in full. It does not mean a thinner mirror of the English page, and it does not mean inventing a local claim the evidence cannot support. Question coverage is still answered, partial, or uncovered.

Short answer

Customer-centric localization means the question a buyer asks in a given language is answered on that language URL, with the same product facts as the other languages. A translated slogan that never states the fit or the limit is not yet an answer.

Fields that must say the same thing

Put the direct answer in the first screen of that language: the product name, the use, and the limit. Then keep company name, specification, and application aligned with the other language. Market-specific intent can add a real question. It should not delete the shared fact.

The issue on the page

A page can be easy to crawl and still fail the buyer. SEO Health asks whether the URL can be fetched and understood. SEO Growth asks which questions in that market are still open. GEO Health asks whether the answer already on the page can be supported. Those tracks stay separate.

Signals that travel with the page

Link from the language’s category or family page to the URL that owns the answer, with anchor text a buyer would recognize. An orphan localized page does not become customer-centric because the prose is polite.

Customer-centric, on this site, means the answer is on the URL the buyer can open. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Write the buyer question in the language of the page.
  • Answer it with product, use, and limit in the first screen.
  • Keep the shared facts aligned and mark coverage answered, partial, or uncovered.
  • Add an in-language link from the page where the question starts.

What this does not claim

Answering a buyer question in another language does not guarantee rankings or AI citations. Mika does not promise inclusion in any answer product.

A practical example

Teaching example, not a case study. A maintenance lead asks which controller firmware matches a named panel. The English page answers with the model and the exclusion. The other language translates the welcome line and links to a contact form. The blueprint writes the same model and exclusion on that URL and re-audits. No customer is named and no metric is claimed.
