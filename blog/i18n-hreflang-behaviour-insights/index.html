Definition

Mika Visibility is an educational and diagnostic product for B2B websites. This note is about hreflang behaviour insights on a multilingual site: one company, parallel language URLs, and the same product facts on each URL. GEO here means generative engine optimization, not geographic targeting. A high health finding never becomes an automatic growth finding.

Short answer

Treat hreflang behaviour insights as a consistency problem first. The company name, the product name, specifications, applications, claims, and the questions you actually answer have to match across languages. Which capacity matches this heat load? should receive the same constraints on every alternate. Market depth can be added. The engineering fact cannot be quietly replaced.

Fields that must say the same thing

Compare six fields before you compare traffic. Company name. Product name, including the model code for chiller. Specifications, especially cooling capacity at stated conditions. Applications, named as process cooling rather than a vague sector word. Claims, including the condition in “capacity is quoted at the same stated conditions”. Question coverage: the buyer question is either answered, partial, or uncovered in that language.

English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Language labels on the homepage illustration include EN, 中文, Español, العربية, Русский, and Deutsch. Those labels are a picture of the idea, not a claim that every label already has a deep market site. Each language you do publish still carries independent SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, and GEO Growth.

The issue on the page

What hreflang actually names

Hreflang is a language and region annotation. On this site the values in use are en, zh-Hans, and x-default. x-default points at the English URL, because English lives at the root and Chinese lives under /zh/. The annotation tells a crawler which URL is the alternate of which other URL. It does not choose a ranking, and it does not invent a market that the page does not actually serve.

A usable cluster is reciprocal. The English page lists the Chinese alternate, and the Chinese page lists the English page back. Each URL also references itself. If one side points at a redirect, a 404, or a canonical that is a different page, the cluster is describing a URL that buyers and crawlers will not stay on. Mika’s technical check looks for that mismatch. It does not guess how often the page will be shown.

Language and region are not the same slot. zh-Hans names the written language. A region code is a separate decision, and only when the page is really aimed at that region. Pointing a thin translated shell at a country does not create coverage. For the chiller, the page that wears the annotation still has to state cooling capacity at stated conditions and process cooling in that language.

Annotations do not repair drifted facts

Return tags can be perfect while the specification table shrinks in translation. Answer systems then see two pages that claim to be alternates and that disagree: capacity is quoted at the same stated conditions. The confusion is a content problem. Hreflang did not cause it, and hreflang will not hide it. The fix is to put the same company name, product name, specifications, applications, and claims on both URLs, then re-check the annotations.

Unfinished translations should not be annotated as if they were equivalents. A noindex on a draft is a cleaner signal than a hreflang entry aimed at a shell. Page count goes up either way. Coverage does not. SEO Growth is the decision to write the missing market page with real depth. GEO Growth is the decision to answer the local question that the shell skipped.

Signals that travel with the page

The language switcher is an internal link with a job: open the alternate of this page, not the home page of the other language. If the control always goes home, product alternates become orphans. Orphans have weak discovery even when they are listed in an XML sitemap.

Units are part of the specification. Converting a range is allowed when the number is actually converted and labeled. Dropping the unit, rounding it into a slogan, or translating only the prose around an unchanged table leaves two values in circulation. Publish one converted value and keep the original value available.

What to check

  • Open the English and Chinese URLs for chiller side by side and read cooling capacity at stated conditions aloud. If one side is shorter, the fields do not match.
  • Confirm reciprocal hreflang among the live canonicals, including a self-reference, with x-default on the English URL.
  • Follow the in-language internal links from the hub to the product page so the alternate is not an orphan.
  • Read the direct answer to “Which capacity matches this heat load?” and check that the condition in “capacity is quoted at the same stated conditions” survived translation.

A practical example

Example, not a customer story. A manufacturer publishes chiller in English and opens /zh/ by translating the menu. The Chinese page shortens cooling capacity at stated conditions and drops the condition in “capacity is quoted at the same stated conditions”. A buyer asking “Which capacity matches this heat load?” finds a fluent page and the wrong limit. Diagnosis separates the issues: GEO Health for the entity drift, SEO Growth for the thin market page, GEO Growth for the unanswered local question. The blueprint restores the specification, writes the direct answer on the product URL, and re-audits. Nothing in that loop promises a ranking.
