A generated localization draft is not the specification

Definition

Generative tools draft from whatever text they are given and from patterns in training. They do not know which sentence on your site is the specification. If the draft smooths a limit into a stronger claim, the page now states something the datasheet does not. Mika Visibility treats that as an evidence problem on the GEO Health track. It does not treat a fluent draft as proof, and it does not claim that ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other product will cite you.

Short answer

A generated localization draft is a proposal for wording, not a second source of product facts. Publish it only after a person checks the product name, the numbers, the units, and the exclusions against the page that already owns those facts.

Fields that must say the same thing

Keep one owner URL for the fact in each language. The reviewer compares company name, product name, specification, application, and the condition attached to any claim. Question coverage stays honest: answered, partial, or uncovered. A draft that sounds complete while omitting the exclusion is partial.

The issue on the page

Do not paste the draft over the HTML and call the job done. The published page has to contain the checked sentence in text, not only inside a chat log or a screenshot. Internal links in that language should point at the datasheet with anchor text that names the document.

Signals that travel with the page

Hreflang still only marks the alternates you actually publish. A generated page that is not yet a real URL should not be added to the sitemap. x-default stays on the English URL. None of these tags promise inclusion in an answer surface.

A chat transcript is not evidence. The evidence is the specification the live page still agrees with. Mika Visibility keeps one set of company, product, specification, and application facts in every language you publish, while the market’s own questions need real depth. English stays at the root. Chinese stays under /zh/. Do not add an /en/ prefix. Hreflang uses en, zh-Hans, and x-default pointing at the English URL. A health finding never becomes a growth score.

What to check

  • Diff the draft against the owner page for every number and unit.
  • Reject any sentence that adds a claim the datasheet does not make.
  • Publish the checked sentence in HTML text.
  • Leave hreflang and the sitemap for URLs that already exist.

What this does not claim

Reviewing a generated draft does not guarantee search rankings or a citation in ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews, or any other answer product. Mika does not sell that placement.

A practical example

Teaching example, not a measured result. A valve page says the seal is suitable up to a stated temperature and not for a named solvent. A draft translation keeps the temperature and drops the solvent exclusion. The fix is to reject that sentence, restore the exclusion, and re-audit the live HTML. The example names no customer and no score.
