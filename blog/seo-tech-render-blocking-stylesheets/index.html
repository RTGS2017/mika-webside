A Render-Blocking Stylesheet Holds the First Paint

Short answer

A stylesheet linked from the head blocks rendering until the browser has it.

That behavior is why CSS can style the first paint without a flash of the wrong layout, and also why an unused sheet delays every page that includes it. The figure of a lab report is a picture of a tool naming blocking resources. It is not a measurement of Mika, of a customer, or of this marketing site, and any number printed inside such a picture is not a result to copy.

Definition

SEO Health asks which sheets are on the product template and which rules those sheets actually apply above the fold. A sheet that exists only to style a homepage hero should not ship in the documentation head. Splitting by template is the repair. Deleting CSS until the page looks broken is not.

What rendering changes for a crawler

Remove or split stylesheets that the sample template does not use for its first screen.

Critical CSS inlined for the first screen can be a follow-up, described in the companion note on inlining. It is not required to justify removing a sheet that does not apply. The audit sample is the HTML head of one URL and the network timing of that sheet, labeled as a lab observation if that is all you have.

What to check

  • Stylesheets linked from the head
  • Sheets that do not apply to the template’s first screen
  • Lab notes labeled as lab notes
  • One sample URL’s head after the unlink
  • No copied score from a tool screenshot

A practical example

Every template links a stylesheet built for a retired campaign. Coverage shows most of it unused on the product page. The health fix is to stop linking it outside the campaign URL. The product page’s own sheet stays. No overall grade is recorded.

What this does not claim

Unblocking a stylesheet does not guarantee rankings or citations.
