Direct answer

A glossary is SEO Growth when a buyer term is actually defined: the word, the product it belongs to, and the limit on how you use it. A page of headings with no sentences is uncovered, not a content asset.

Why it matters

Terms drift. Sales, the datasheet, and the menu may use three labels for one part. The glossary’s job is to pick the preferred label and point the aliases at it. That is entity clarity, recorded here as a coverage task because the term was previously uncovered. It does not require a separate URL per synonym if one page can list them without becoming a thin duplicate set.

What to check

Choose terms from real buyer questions and from the product pages. Write one sentence that a stranger can use. Link the product URL that uses the term. Do not define a term you do not sell by borrowing another company’s wording.

  • Prefer one label and list aliases under it.
  • Tie the term to a product you actually describe.
  • Link the product page.
  • Do not mint empty term URLs.

A practical example

The menu says “module,” the drawing says “cell,” and no page says they are the same part. The glossary sentence states the preferred name and the alias, then links the product URL. No dictionary rank is claimed.

Content depth

One accurate sentence plus the link is depth for a narrow term. A thousand-word essay that never says which product you mean is not.

How an unanswered topic gets found

Link the term from the product page with the preferred name as the anchor. Keep one glossary URL if the terms are short, or one URL per term only when each has its own fact. Re-audit that the sentence is in HTML.
