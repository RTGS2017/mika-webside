An Entity Graph from the Company to the Evidence

Entity clarity starts with who the company is and ends where a reader can check the claim.

Educational material only. Mika does not guarantee search rankings or AI answer citations.

Definition

The entity graph links the company to products, then to applications, technical information, documentation, and evidence. A reader should be able to move from who the company is to what it sells, where those products apply, which technical details support them, which documents record them, and which evidence sits next to the claim. Five dimensions are named only, with no sample scores: entity clarity, answerability, evidence coverage, consistency, and citation readiness.

Short answer

Company pages should say who you are, what you sell, and where you operate, without hype. Product pages should carry the specifications, use cases, and proof a buyer needs before contact, with verifiable context placed next to each claim.

What to check

  • Link each product to the company
  • Link applications and documents
  • Place evidence beside claims
  • Name the five dimensions only

What this does not claim

A complete entity graph does not guarantee that a model will recommend the company or cite its documents. Naming citation readiness among the dimensions does not guarantee a citation, and those dimensions are not published here as sample scores.

A practical example

A specification can connect a product to an application, then to a document, then to the evidence for a stated constraint. Consistency means those facts agree across pages and languages, which is a dimension to inspect rather than a number to display.
