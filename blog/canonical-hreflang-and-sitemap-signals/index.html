Canonical, Hreflang, and Sitemap Signals

Read the canonical, the hreflang alternates, and the XML sitemap together, because each signal can name a different URL.

Definition

A canonical element points at the intended indexable URL, hreflang names language alternates, and an XML sitemap lists URLs the site offers for crawling. The sitemap can disagree with indexable URLs on coverage, freshness, or indexation, so the audit records that disagreement as a signal.

Short answer

Compare the canonical URL, the hreflang alternates for en, zh-Hans, and x-default to English, and the URLs present in the sitemap. Hreflang names those alternates without guessing outcomes, and these checks are audit signals rather than levers that force a result.

What to check

  • Canonical matches the indexable URL
  • Hreflang lists en and zh-Hans
  • X-default points to the English URL
  • Sitemap coverage can disagree

What this does not claim

Aligning canonical, hreflang, and sitemap signals does not guarantee indexation or citation. A deep audit groups these findings by rule with sample URLs, and it does not guarantee a single combined outcome.

A practical example

English at the site root and Chinese under /zh/ can be named with hreflang en, zh-Hans, and x-default to English, with no /en/ prefix. If the sitemap still lists a retired URL while the canonical points at the current page, the signals disagree and the audit records that mismatch.
