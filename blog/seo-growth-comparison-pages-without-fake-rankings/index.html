Direct answer

A comparison page is useful when it states differences a buyer can check: range, material, or exclusion. It is not useful when it invents a rank, a star score, or a competitor metric. SEO Growth does not need a fake league table.

Why it matters

Differentiation is a stated difference, not a medal. If you name another product, do not attribute a number you did not publish as your own fact, and do not paste their logo as if they were a customer. The safe comparison is your configuration against your other configuration, with the datasheet beside the sentence. Question coverage is whether the buyer’s “which one” is answered.

What to check

Pick one question, such as which seal suits a named temperature. Answer with your options and the limits. Link each option to its specification. Refuse columns that say “best” without a stated criterion you can support.

  • State the criterion in words a buyer can check.
  • Use your own documented limits.
  • Do not invent ranks, awards, or competitor metrics.
  • Link the datasheets the table depends on.

A practical example

A table ranks three seals “1, 2, 3” with no criterion. The repair replaces the ranks with temperature limits from the three datasheets and links those files. No market-share figure is added.

Content depth

Depth is the criterion and the evidence. A grid of checkmarks without units is still a slogan. The answer can be a short table whose columns name real fields.

How an unanswered topic gets found

One URL should own the comparison. Link it from the product pages it mentions, with anchors that name the choice. Do not publish a copy for every city. Re-audit the table in HTML.
