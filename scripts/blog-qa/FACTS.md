## Four tracks (homepage framework)

- One website. Four independent capabilities. SEO and GEO stay on separate tracks. Health and Growth stay on separate scales.
- SEO Health (Now · Search): Is the live site technically ready to be found and understood in search? Tags: Technical, Structure, Content.
- GEO Health (Now · Answers): Can people find a clear, supportable answer about the business from the site itself? Tags: Understanding, Evidence, Answers.
- SEO Growth (Next · Search): Which coverage, intent depth, and markets are still unoccupied? Tags: Coverage, Intent, Depth, Markets.
- GEO Growth (Next · Answers): Where can answer coverage, citation readiness, and expansion still improve? Tags: Query gaps, Answer gaps, Citation, Expansion. "Citation" here means readiness, not a promised citation.
- A site can be healthy and still be hard to discover.

## Audit path

- Deep Audit sees the site as a system, not a single score.
- A scan walks pages, structure, topics, entities, questions, and evidence before any blueprint is written.
- Path: Audit → Diagnosis → Blueprint. Do not stop at findings. Ship the next action.
- Loop: SCAN, DIAGNOSE, BLUEPRINT, OPTIMIZE, RE-AUDIT. Validate progress with a re-audit after changes ship.
- SEO health pass groups findings by rule with sample URLs: crawlability, architecture, on-page basics, internal links, and related technical checks.
- A website SEO audit reviews how search engines can crawl, index, and understand pages. It does not guess a future ranking.

## GEO

- GEO means Generative Engine Optimization. It is not geographic SEO.
- Modern search often shows direct answers, summaries, and source links — not only blue links.
- GEO checks: clear company and product entities; direct answers to buyer questions; evidence tied to claims; consistent context across key pages and languages.
- GEO measures whether information is understandable, supportable, and referenceable. It does not guarantee inclusion, recommendation, or citation in any AI product (including ChatGPT, Gemini, or AI Overviews).
- Answer path: Question → Topic → Intent → Entity → Evidence → Direct Answer.
- Four building blocks: Entities (stable names and facts), Direct answers (short plain responses), Evidence (specs, process, verifiable context), Consistent context (same core facts across About, product, and language versions).
- Citation readiness means a careful reader could quote a statement with less ambiguity. It is a quality of the page, not a promise of placement.
- Entity graph on the homepage: Company → Products, Applications, Technical Information, Documentation, Evidence.
- Named dimensions (names only, no scores): entity clarity, answerability, evidence coverage, consistency, citation readiness.
- Company information should state who you are, what you sell, and where you operate — without hype.
- Teaching example already on the homepage (illustrative, not a customer): the question "Which product is suitable for low-rise concrete placement?" and the suggested action "Add a direct answer block on the relevant product page with use case, constraints, and a link to specifications."
- Hypothetical contrast already used in the knowledge center: a page of slogans versus a page that names the company, product families, applications, and an operating range with a datasheet next to claims. Do not name a customer.

## Questions and intent

- If buyer questions are missing, partial, or buried, discovery and trust stall even when the site looks technically fine.
- Question coverage states: Answered, Partial, Uncovered. Commercial intents are a separate cut: total, covered, partial, missing. Describe the categories only.
- Search intent types already used on the site: informational, commercial, transactional.
- Content gaps mean buyer questions the site does not actually answer.
- Topic coverage means depth without manufacturing near-duplicate pages.
- Commercial search opportunities are queries the product pages can honestly support.
- Product pages should carry specs, use cases, and proof buyers need before contact.
- Page count is not SEO growth. More URLs are not the same as findable, useful coverage.
- Near-duplicates and thin template collisions are a duplicate-content problem.

## Technical checks already described

- Crawlability: robots rules, status codes, and crawl paths.
- Broken internal links waste crawl paths and user trust.
- Orphan pages have no internal links and weak discovery paths.
- Canonical tags should point at the intended indexable URL.
- Hreflang checks language and region alternates. It does not guess rankings.
- XML sitemaps are read for coverage, freshness, and indexation mismatches.
- Each new blog and Q&A on this site is its own HTML document with a canonical URL, so it can be listed in the sitemap and linked with real anchors.
