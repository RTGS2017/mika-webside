Definition

Intent still matters. An informational question wants a definition. A commercial question wants fit. A transactional question wants the next honest step. The entity is which company and product the intent applies to. Evidence is what supports the sentence. The direct answer is the sentence itself. Collapsing these into one slogan loses the path.

Short answer

The useful order is question, then topic, then intent, then the entity, then evidence, then the direct answer. A paragraph that answers “which one” without naming the product has skipped the entity. GEO keeps this path separate from a ranking method.

What belongs on the page

Write the question as the heading or the first line. Name the product in the answer. Match the intent so a definition is not forced into a fake cart. Attach evidence. Then stop, instead of adding unrelated claims.

Each step can be a few words. The path fails when a step is missing, not when the page is shorter than an essay. Question coverage states remain answered, partial, and uncovered.

This is the answer path GEO describes. It is not a ranking recipe. In Mika Visibility, GEO means generative engine optimization, not geographic targeting. The four building blocks are entities, direct answers, evidence, and consistent context. Citation readiness means a careful reader could quote a statement with less ambiguity. It is a quality of the page, not a promise that any answer product will cite it.

What to check

  • State the question first.
  • Name the entity in the answer.
  • Match informational, commercial, or transactional intent.
  • End with evidence and the direct answer.

A practical example

Question: which seal suits the stated temperature? A page answers “the premium range” and never names a seal or a limit. The repair names the seal family, the temperature, and the datasheet, in that order. No query volume is required for the example.
