Short answer

Same facts across languages means the English page at the root and the Chinese page under /zh/ state one company, one product, one specification, and one application. Hreflang marks the alternates. It does not decide which wording is true.

What belongs on the page

Read the specification aloud in both languages. Check company name, product name, unit, and exclusion. Set hreflang to en, zh-Hans, and x-default on the English URL. Do not add /en/. Fix the text before you celebrate the tags.

Parity is factual, not stylistic. Idiom can differ. A number cannot quietly change. If a fact exists only in English, the Chinese page is partial or uncovered for that question.

Same facts across languages is consistent context applied to the bilingual site. In Mika Visibility, GEO means generative engine optimization, not geographic targeting. The four building blocks are entities, direct answers, evidence, and consistent context. Citation readiness means a careful reader could quote a statement with less ambiguity. It is a quality of the page, not a promise that any answer product will cite it.

What to check

  • Compare the specification, not only the title.
  • Keep /zh/ for Chinese and the root for English.
  • Use en, zh-Hans, and x-default.
  • Mark coverage per language when it differs.

A practical example

English states 2 m. Chinese states 2 and drops the unit, then hreflang looks perfect. The repair restores 2 m on the Chinese page. The tags were never the defect. No language-market share is claimed.
