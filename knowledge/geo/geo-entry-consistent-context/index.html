Definition

The fields Mika compares across languages are company name, product name, specifications, applications, claims, and question coverage. Context is consistent when those fields do not fork. A high SEO Health score does not repair a product page that names a different range from the About page. Health and growth stay independent, and GEO stays independent of both.

Short answer

Consistent context means the key pages state the same company name, product name, specification, and application. GEO checks that agreement. A fluent contradiction is still a contradiction. There is no blended score that hides it.

What belongs on the page

Pick the sentence that is the source of the fact. Make About, the product URL, and the other language repeat it. Where a market adds a true local note, label it as additional, not as a replacement.

Consistency is not sameness of adjectives. Pages can be different lengths. They cannot assign two operating ranges to one model. Partial coverage is the honest label when one page has the range and another alludes to it.

Consistent context is the fourth GEO building block, beside entities, direct answers, and evidence. In Mika Visibility, GEO means generative engine optimization, not geographic targeting. The four building blocks are entities, direct answers, evidence, and consistent context. Citation readiness means a careful reader could quote a statement with less ambiguity. It is a quality of the page, not a promise that any answer product will cite it.

What to check

  • Compare company, product, specification, and application.
  • Treat a contradictory translation as a forked fact.
  • Label true local notes as additional.
  • Do not average the disagreement into one score.

A practical example

About says the firm sells sensors rated to a stated temperature. The product page omits the temperature. The Chinese page invents a higher one. The repair makes all three state the original rating. No consistency percentage is claimed.
