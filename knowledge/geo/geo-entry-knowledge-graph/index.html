Definition

A knowledge graph is a set of named things and the relationships between them, such as a company, its products, their applications, and the documents that support a claim. In Generative Engine Optimization this is a clarity problem, not a geographic-SEO trick and not a ranking lever. GEO keeps SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, and GEO Growth on separate tracks. A page can be easy to crawl and still leave how the company, the product, and the proof connect unresolved.

How this shows up on a website

Answer systems do not receive a private briefing from sales. They read sentences, names, tables, and links that the HTML actually contains. When the text never settles which facts connect the company to its products and proof, the summary has to guess how the company, the product, and the proof connect. That guess is a failure of the page, even when the site is technically reachable.

A knowledge graph is a set of named things and the relationships between them, such as a company, its products, their applications, and the documents that support a claim. The practical move is small and specific: the company, the product families, the applications, and the documents as separate named nodes, with links between them. Put that passage where a buyer lands, not only in a slogan line or a title attribute. Then connect it to an entity path from the company page to a product page, then to a specification or other openable document, so the claim and the support are on speaking terms.

Mika’s teaching graph runs from the company to products, applications, technical information, documentation, and evidence. Those are names of relationships, not scores. A page that collapses all five into one slogan gives a summarizer a single blob. Name the nodes, then name the edge: this product is used for this application, and this document holds the range.

Question coverage still has three honest states: answered, partial, and uncovered. A page that names the topic but never answers which facts connect the company to its products and proof is partial at best. A site that never asks the question at all is uncovered. GEO Growth is the place to record that gap. GEO Health is the place to record whether the pages you already have can support an answer. Neither scale is an overall blended score.

What to check

Say the name in the body

Put the concrete name in a sentence a stranger can read without opening a menu. The first screen should state the company, the product families, the applications, and the documents as separate named nodes, with links between them. A heading that only says “solutions” does not identify the entity, and a model number buried in a download does not help a summary that never sees the sentence.

Answer the question in one passage

The question to answer in plain language is: which facts connect the company to its products and proof? One short passage should be quotable on its own. If the passage still leaves how the company, the product, and the proof connect, mark the coverage partial and add the missing constraint, range, or exclusion instead of another adjective.

Put evidence next to the claim

The evidence for this page is an entity path from the company page to a product page, then to a specification or other openable document. Link it with anchor text that names the destination, and make sure the target URL returns the document you mean. A claim with no openable support is not citation-ready, even if the sentence sounds confident.

Repeat the same fact in both languages

English at the site root and Chinese under /zh/ should keep the same company name, product name, specification, and application. Translation may change word order. It should not invent a second a knowledge graph or a second operating range. Hreflang marks the alternates. It does not guess which language an answer product will prefer.

A practical example

If every page says the brand builds systems, nothing records that a pump family is for cooling water and that the curve lives in a named datasheet. Writing those three links is the graph. The contrast used across Mika’s teaching material is the same kind of contrast: a page of slogans versus a page that names the company, the product family, the application, and a constraint, with a datasheet beside the claim. That scene is illustrative. It is not a customer story, and it is not a measured result.
