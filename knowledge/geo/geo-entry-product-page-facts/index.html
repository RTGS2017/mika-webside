Definition

A product is the thing a buyer can specify: a named model or family with an application and a limit. In Generative Engine Optimization this is a clarity problem, not a geographic-SEO trick and not a ranking lever. GEO keeps SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, and GEO Growth on separate tracks. Short answer

A product matters here because a person, or a system writing a short answer from your site, still has to settle what is being sold and under which constraints. The useful page states the name, the application, the constraint, and the specification link. Proof sits with that statement as the specification for that name. Citation readiness means a careful reader could attribute that statement with less ambiguity. It describes the page. It does not promise that any answer product will quote it.

How this shows up on a website

Answer systems do not receive a private briefing from sales. They read sentences, names, tables, and links that the HTML actually contains. When the text never settles what is being sold and under which constraints, the summary has to guess the name of the thing being sold. That guess is a failure of the page, even when the site is technically reachable.

A product is the thing a buyer can specify: a named model or family with an application and a limit. The practical move is small and specific: the name, the application, the constraint, and the specification link. Put that passage where a buyer lands, not only in a slogan line or a title attribute. Then connect it to the specification for that name, so the claim and the support are on speaking terms.

Commercial intent belongs on pages that can honestly support it. Informational pages can define the category. Transactional details, if you publish them, must match the product you named. Do not create a product URL that only repeats the category slogan. That URL is thin, and more thin URLs are not growth.

Question coverage still has three honest states: answered, partial, and uncovered. A page that names the topic but never answers what is being sold and under which constraints is partial at best. A site that never asks the question at all is uncovered. GEO Growth is the place to record that gap. GEO Health is the place to record whether the pages you already have can support an answer. Neither scale is an overall blended score.

What to check

Say the name in the body

Put the concrete name in a sentence a stranger can read without opening a menu. The first screen should state the name, the application, the constraint, and the specification link. A heading that only says “solutions” does not identify the entity, and a model number buried in a download does not help a summary that never sees the sentence.

Answer the question in one passage

The question to answer in plain language is: what is being sold and under which constraints? One short passage should be quotable on its own. If the passage still leaves the name of the thing being sold, mark the coverage partial and add the missing constraint, range, or exclusion instead of another adjective.

Put evidence next to the claim

The evidence for this page is the specification for that name. Link it with anchor text that names the destination, and make sure the target URL returns the document you mean. A claim with no openable support is not citation-ready, even if the sentence sounds confident.

Repeat the same fact in both languages

English at the site root and Chinese under /zh/ should keep the same company name, product name, specification, and application. Translation may change word order. It should not invent a second a product or a second operating range. Hreflang marks the alternates. It does not guess which language an answer product will prefer.

A practical example

A page titled Solutions does not name a product. A page titled NV-40 isolation valve, for low-pressure water, not for steam, with a datasheet, does. The contrast used across Mika’s teaching material is the same kind of contrast: a page of slogans versus a page that names the company, the product family, the application, and a constraint, with a datasheet beside the claim. That scene is illustrative. It is not a customer story, and it is not a measured result.
