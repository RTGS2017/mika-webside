Definition

Company information should state who you are, what you sell, and where you operate, without hype. The product page should say which application that product is for, and the constraint. Documentation and evidence come next. If any of the three names is missing, a summary has to guess. GEO Health records that gap. It does not assign the homepage’s illustrative entity scores.

Short answer

A page is easier to understand when it names the company, the product, and the application in ordinary sentences. That trio is the start of the entity graph. It is not a tagline, and it is not a customer logo wall.

What belongs on the page

Write one sentence a stranger can read: the company sells this product for this application, within this constraint. Use it on the About page and the product page, in both languages. Link each to the others with those names as anchors.

The application should be specific enough to exclude a wrong use. “Industry” is not an application. A named process or environment is. Do not invent a flagship customer to make the application feel real.

Company, product, and application are the first links in the entity graph. In Mika Visibility, GEO means generative engine optimization, not geographic targeting. The four building blocks are entities, direct answers, evidence, and consistent context. Citation readiness means a careful reader could quote a statement with less ambiguity. It is a quality of the page, not a promise that any answer product will cite it.

What to check

  • Name the company, the product, and the application.
  • Add the constraint in the same passage.
  • Keep the trio aligned across languages.
  • Do not substitute a slogan or a logo wall.

A practical example

“We empower operations” names nobody. The repair says North Line sells the CX-4 controller for panel monitoring within a stated temperature. The names are a teaching shape, not a real customer.
