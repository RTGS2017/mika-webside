How this shows up on a website

Answer systems do not receive a private briefing from sales. They read sentences, names, tables, and links that the HTML actually contains. When the text never settles what the figure shows if the image cannot be seen, the summary has to guess the name of what the figure shows. That guess is a failure of the page, even when the site is technically reachable.

What to check

Say the name in the body

Put the concrete name in a sentence a stranger can read without opening a menu. The first screen should state a short description of the diagram or photo in the page language. A heading that only says “solutions” does not identify the entity, and a model number buried in a download does not help a summary that never sees the sentence.

Answer the question in one passage

The question to answer in plain language is: what the figure shows if the image cannot be seen? One short passage should be quotable on its own. If the passage still leaves the name of what the figure shows, mark the coverage partial and add the missing constraint, range, or exclusion instead of another adjective.

Put evidence next to the claim

The evidence for this page is the figure caption that points at the source or the specification. Link it with anchor text that names the destination, and make sure the target URL returns the document you mean. A claim with no openable support is not citation-ready, even if the sentence sounds confident.

Repeat the same fact in both languages

English at the site root and Chinese under /zh/ should keep the same company name, product name, specification, and application. Translation may change word order. It should not invent a second alternative text or a second operating range. Hreflang marks the alternates. It does not guess which language an answer product will prefer.

A practical example

A photo of the NV-40 nameplate should say NV-40 nameplate showing the model and revision, not picture. A decorative swoosh can be empty alt. A spec diagram cannot. The contrast used across Mika’s teaching material is the same kind of contrast: a page of slogans versus a page that names the company, the product family, the application, and a constraint, with a datasheet beside the claim. That scene is illustrative. It is not a customer story, and it is not a measured result.
