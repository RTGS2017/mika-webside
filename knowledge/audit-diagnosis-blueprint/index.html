Audit, Diagnosis, Blueprint

Scan produces facts. Diagnosis groups them. Blueprint turns groups into prioritized work you can re-check.

Direct answer

A useful audit does not end when the crawler finishes. After the scan you still need discovery (structured facts), diagnosis (grouped meaning), a blueprint (prioritized page actions), and a re-audit (validation). That sequence keeps SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, and GEO Growth as independent capabilities — never one blended overall score.

Health findings tell you whether foundations are usable. Growth findings tell you what demand is still unanswered. The blueprint should say which job each action serves, with a clear check after publish. Blueprint is guidance. It does not auto-edit or auto-publish your site.

Why it matters

Deep Audit sees the site as a system — not a single score. A scan walks pages, structure, topics, entities, questions, and evidence before any blueprint is written. Stopping at a raw finding list leaves teams with noise. Stopping at a story without page actions leaves teams without owners. Shipping without re-audit leaves teams guessing.

The product loop ends on Re-audit, not “Done.” Progress is checked against the same independent lenses: crawl/index health, coverage growth, answer clarity, and unanswered questions. Mika does not sell rankings or guaranteed answer-surface placement.

The process

Homepage framing: Audit → Diagnosis → Blueprint. In practice the full loop is:

  1. Scan / Discover — Walk pages, structure, topics, entities, questions, and evidence. Record sample URLs and concrete observations, not a vibe score.
  2. Diagnose — Group issues by rule and severity. Separate crawl/index blockers from coverage gaps, and unclear entities from unanswered questions. Keep the four capabilities labeled.
  3. Blueprint — Convert groups into page-level actions: target path, suggested changes, priority, and a validation step such as re-audit. Types may include technical fixes, expand_page, or ADD_EVIDENCE-style work — always with execute: false in spirit: the plan does not auto-edit the site.
  4. Act — Your team publishes the chosen changes.
  5. Re-audit — Scan again. Confirm what improved, what remains, and what new opportunities appeared.

Discovery without diagnosis leaves noise. Diagnosis without a blueprint leaves stories. A blueprint without re-audit leaves guessing. The loop is the product of the audit, not a decorative report cover.

What a blueprint contains

  • Target URL — the page that should own the fix.
  • Capability label — SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, or GEO Growth.
  • Suggested changes — for example definition, use case, specifications, evidence, FAQ, or internal-link repair.
  • Priority — P0/P1-style sequencing so foundation blockers are not buried under nice-to-have coverage.
  • Validation — what re-audit should confirm after publish.

Know where your website stands. Know what to do next. Run a structured SEO + GEO audit to get health findings, growth gaps, and a blueprint you can act on — without a black-box overall score.

Example

Discovery might flag a product URL with a thin template, missing use case, and no FAQ — alongside a separate health note about orphaned documentation pages. Diagnosis puts the orphan under SEO Health (discovery path) and the thin product under SEO Growth / GEO Growth (coverage and answerability), without averaging them.

A blueprint action could look like: expand /products/example/ with a product definition, use case, specifications, evidence, and FAQ; priority P1; validate with re-audit. A second action reconnects the orphan docs into the hub. Each action states its job. Neither claims a ranking or citation outcome.

When the team publishes, re-audit checks whether the product page now answers the intended questions and whether the docs are reachable. Health and growth panels update independently — a healthier link graph does not invent finished topic coverage by itself.

FAQ

What should happen after a website scan?

Do not stop at a list of findings. Move through discovery (facts with sample URLs), diagnosis (group by rule, severity, and opportunity), blueprint (page-level actions with priority and validation), and re-audit after changes ship.

Why is a single overall score a poor ending for an audit?

SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, and GEO Growth answer different questions. Blending them hides whether you need crawl fixes, coverage work, clearer entities, or new answers. A blueprint should keep those tracks visible.

Does a blueprint automatically change my live site?

No. A blueprint is guidance: target URL, suggested changes, priority, and a re-audit check. Your team decides what to publish. The value is a sequenced plan grounded in diagnosed findings — not auto-editing or ranking promises.

When is the audit “done”?

It is not. The continuous loop ends on re-audit, which can surface remaining health issues and new growth opportunities. Treat each cycle as progress against independent panels, not a one-time certificate.
