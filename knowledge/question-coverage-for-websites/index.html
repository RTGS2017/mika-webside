Question Coverage for Websites

Find the real questions your site has not answered yet — then put direct answers on the pages that should own them.

Direct answer

Question coverage is the practice of comparing real buyer questions with what your website actually answers. The goal is to find what is answered, partial, or uncovered — not to invent a keyword count as a growth metric.

Uncovered questions are growth signals for both SEO Growth and GEO Growth. They do not replace SEO Health or GEO Health. A crawlable site can still leave important pre-contact questions unanswered. High health never automatically means finished question coverage.

How coverage works

Start from questions people ask before they contact sales: which product fits a constraint, what specs matter, what proof exists, how markets differ, why a page should be trusted. Map each question to a candidate page.

  • Answered — a direct answer exists on the owning page, with enough context to act.
  • Partial — related copy exists but skips constraints, proof, or a clear recommendation path.
  • Uncovered — the question only appears in chat, email, or a competitor’s page.

After the scan, move through discovery, diagnosis, blueprint, and re-audit. Diagnosis groups commercial and informational gaps. The blueprint names the target URL and the answer block to add. Re-audit checks whether the question moved from uncovered or partial to answered — without blending health and growth into one score.

Keep the four capabilities visible. SEO Health still matters if discovery paths are broken. GEO Health still matters if entities and evidence are unclear. Question coverage mainly feeds growth: which demand remains open.

Example

A buyer asks: “Which product is suitable for low-rise concrete placement?” The catalog lists models and slogans. Specs exist, but no page states suitability, constraints, and a link to documentation in one short answer block.

Coverage marks the question as uncovered or partial. The blueprint action expands the relevant product page with a direct answer, use case, constraints, and a specifications link. That is growth work on a specific URL — not a promise that the page will rank or be cited, and not a claim that overall SEO “got healthier” by averaging panels.

FAQ

What will people ask before they contact us?

Buyers usually ask about fit, constraints, specifications, proof, lead time, and how to choose between options. Question coverage maps those real questions to pages that answer them directly. If answers live only in sales chat, the website still has uncovered demand.

Why do competitors show up for searches we should cover?

Often the site is technically fine but leaves commercial or application questions partial or missing. That is a growth and coverage problem, not proof that health failed. Finding uncovered questions helps you decide which pages to expand before you assume a ranking contest.

Where should buyer questions appear on the site?

Put short, direct answers on the product or application page that should own the question — with links to specs and evidence. A buried PDF or a homepage slogan rarely counts as coverage. Blueprint actions should name the target URL and the answer block to add.
