Entity, Evidence & Answerability

Three building blocks of trustworthy website answers: stable names, supportable claims, and direct replies.

Direct answer

Entities are the stable things your site talks about — company, products, applications, documents. Evidence is the support behind claims. Answerability is whether a page gives a direct reply to a real question.

Together they strengthen GEO Health: clearer understanding and reference readiness on the live site. They also support growth when missing answers or thin proof become blueprint actions. They do not create an overall score with SEO Health or SEO Growth, and they do not guarantee citations or rankings. Clear information is easier to trust, understand, and reference.

Why it matters

People and modern search surfaces need stable entities, direct answers, and claims that point to evidence. A page that only repeats slogans forces readers — and any summarizer — to invent meaning. A page that names the company, defines the product, answers selection questions, and points to proof gives systems and people something concrete.

Mika tracks evidence coverage, consistency, and citation readiness, then blueprints concrete pages to expand definitions, specs, use cases, and FAQ blocks. That work improves readiness. It does not promise placement in ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews, or any other answer product.

The three blocks

  • Entity clarity — Consistent company and product names, relationships (products under company, applications under products), and facts that do not contradict across pages or languages. Entity and evidence checks score clarity, consistency, and citation readiness across company, product, application, and documentation nodes.
  • Evidence coverage — Specs, process notes, documentation, and other checkable context attached to claims. Prefer a Claim → Evidence → Citation path. Mark UNKNOWN slogans as do-not-invent; do not fabricate commercial facts.
  • Answerability — Short, findable answers on the owning page, not only long prose or marketing narrative.

Relate these to the four independent capabilities. Weak entities and evidence are primarily GEO Health issues. Missing questions are GEO Growth (and often SEO Growth) opportunities. Crawl and architecture issues remain SEO Health. Topic and market gaps remain SEO Growth. Keep panels separate after diagnosis so the blueprint assigns the right job.

How to check

  1. List preferred legal and product names; sample About, product, and language mirrors for conflicts.
  2. Pick top commercial claims; require a nearby spec, process note, or document link for each.
  3. Write five real buyer questions; confirm each has a two-to-five sentence answer on an owning URL.
  4. Flag multilingual drift: same SKU, different facts, or missing evidence in one language.
  5. Blueprint ADD_EVIDENCE or expand_page actions with target URLs and a re-audit check.

Example

A product page says “industry-leading durability” with no measurable attribute, no test context, and no link to documentation. The entity (product name) may be clear, but evidence and answerability are thin. A buyer asking “What duty cycle is this rated for?” finds a slogan instead of a reply.

Diagnosis labels the claim as unsupported and the duty-cycle question as uncovered. The blueprint adds a definition, rated attributes, a documentation link, and a two-sentence direct answer. Re-audit checks extractability and consistency — still without promising that any answer surface will quote the page.

FAQ

How do I know if my company information is clear enough?

A visitor should be able to state who you are, what you sell, and where you operate from the site itself — with consistent names across key pages and languages. If those facts conflict or hide behind slogans, entity clarity is weak even when the site crawls well.

What information is missing from my product pages?

Many product pages list features but skip definitions, use cases, specifications, proof, and short answers to selection questions. Adding those blocks improves answerability and evidence coverage without guaranteeing that any system will cite them.

What proof should support claims on the site?

Tie claims to specs, process descriptions, documentation, or other verifiable context on or linked from the page. Vague marketing adjectives without evidence weaken trust for people and for answer surfaces that look for supportable statements.

Do entities replace technical SEO?

No. Clear entities help interpretation of reachable pages. Crawlability, indexing, and internal paths remain SEO Health work. Mika keeps SEO and GEO on separate tracks.
