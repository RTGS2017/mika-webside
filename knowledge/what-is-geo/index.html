What Is GEO?

GEO means Generative Engine Optimization: making company and product pages clear enough to support direct answers, evidence, and citation readiness—without promising placement in any AI product.

Definition

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization. In practical website work, it means preparing your pages so people—and the systems that summarize or cite sources—can understand who you are, what you sell, what is claimed, and what evidence supports those claims.

GEO is not a magic ranking lever. It does not replace technical SEO, content quality, or crawlability. It focuses on answerability: clear entities, direct answers, evidence, and consistent context across key pages and languages.

Short answer

GEO asks whether your website can support a clear answer about your business. If a buyer asks what you make, where you operate, how a product differs, or what proof you publish, can a page state that without guesswork? Clearer pages are easier to understand and reference. Results in any specific answer product are never guaranteed.

How GEO works on a website

Modern search experiences often show short answers, summaries, and source links—not only a list of blue links. That change raises the bar for clarity. A page that only repeats slogans leaves little for a summary to rest on. A page that names the company, defines the product, answers selection questions, and points to proof gives systems and people something concrete to work with.

Four building blocks show up again and again:

  • Entities — stable names and facts for the company, products, places, and people that matter.
  • Direct answers — short, plain responses to real buyer questions near the top of relevant pages.
  • Evidence — specifications, certifications, case details, or other support tied to claims.
  • Consistent context — the same core facts across About, product, and language versions.

Together, these improve what we call citation readiness: the page is structured so a careful reader could quote or attribute a statement with less ambiguity. Citation readiness is a quality of the content, not a promise that ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overviews, or any other surface will cite you.

A practical example

Imagine two industrial equipment sites. Site A says “world-class solutions for global partners” on every page. Site B states the company name, founding location, product families, typical applications, and a measurable operating range for each model, with a downloadable datasheet next to performance claims.

When someone asks which supplier fits a specific duty cycle, Site B gives a usable answer surface. Site A forces the reader—or any summarizer—to invent meaning. GEO work is largely the craft of moving from Site A toward Site B without inventing marketing claims you cannot support.

GEO and SEO Health / Growth

GEO is not a separate website and not an overall score that replaces SEO. Mika keeps SEO Health, SEO Growth, GEO Health, and GEO Growth on separate tracks. SEO Health still covers crawl access, index signals, and technical foundations. SEO Growth still covers intent coverage, content gaps, and commercial opportunities.

GEO Health looks at whether entities, answers, evidence, and context are clear enough to be understood. GEO Growth looks at unanswered buyer questions and thin answer surfaces that could be improved. Strong SEO makes pages reachable; strong GEO makes the reachable pages easier to interpret. You need both. One does not substitute for the other, and blending them into a single “total score” hides where the real fix lives.

FAQ

What does GEO actually check on a website?

GEO-oriented review looks for clear company and product entities, direct answers to buyer questions, evidence next to claims, and consistent facts across key pages. It measures whether information is understandable and supportable—not whether a specific AI product will show you.

Is GEO a replacement for SEO?

No. Crawlability, indexing, titles, internal links, and intent coverage still matter. GEO builds on that foundation by making reachable pages clearer for answers and citations. Mika keeps SEO and GEO on separate tracks with no blended overall score.

Why might a competitor show up in answers about my category?

Often because their pages name the product, state use cases, and publish proof in a form that is easier to summarize. Your site may still rank for some queries while leaving the direct-answer surface thin. Fixing that starts with clearer entities and questions—not with promising guaranteed citations.
